Statistical Methods in Linguistics

The question

The first formant (F1) is an acoustic correlate of vowel height: higher vowels tend to have lower F1. We will ask whether F1 differs between /i/ as in heed and /ɪ/ as in hid in the Hillenbrand vowel sample.

Every recorded speaker produced both vowels. Thus the theoretically relevant predictor is vowel category, while speaker identifies which two measurements form a pair. The focal method is inference for a paired mean difference: construct one within-speaker difference and estimate its population mean.

By the end of the assignment, you should be able to (i) identify a contrast-bearing unit from a repeated design, (ii) compute and interpret a paired t interval, and (iii) explain why an analysis that ignores the pairing answers the question less efficiently.

NoteReading

Read Hillenbrand, Getty, Clark, and Wheeler (1995), “Acoustic characteristics of American English vowels”. Focus on the speaker sample, recording procedure, and formant measurements. The reading is included because the paired analysis follows from the recording design: each speaker contributes both vowel measurements, and the within-speaker contrast is the unit that bears on vowel category.

Before beginning, review turning linguistic records into data, paired observations, and inference for a paired mean difference. These pages establish the unit of analysis, the pairing relation, and the confidence interval used below.

Data and source

The data derive from Hillenbrand, Getty, Clark, and Wheeler’s study of American English vowels (1995, JASA) and are loaded from the phonTools::h95 teaching table. The preparation script retains all 12 vowels for 139 speakers. You will select the two vowels required by the question.
