# Transcript design

The course transcript is an instructional companion to the slides, not a second set of notes. Each numbered transcript entry corresponds to one slide and uses the same title, dataset, mathematical object, and inferential claim.

## Classroom voice

The delivery begins by orienting students to the object already visible on screen. It then names the operation, reads the relevant pattern from the data, and states the licensed interpretation. A short check or transition closes the entry when it helps students anticipate the next step.

The transcript uses ordinary classroom discourse: `look first at`, `notice`, `compare`, `what changes`, and `what does not change`. It avoids stage directions, generic motivational language, and claims that are stronger than the data. Equations are introduced through the linguistic quantities they represent before their notation is read aloud.

## Empirical and visual discipline

All numerical claims come from the corresponding notes analysis. When the slide contains a plot, the transcript tells the instructor what pattern to locate rather than redescribing every mark. When a model is fitted, the transcript distinguishes observed data, fitted values, and uncertainty. Dataset introductions retain the citation and source link used in the notes.

## Alignment

The slide generator writes the speaker notes and standalone transcript from the same record. The alignment validator then checks the slide identifiers, notes-page digests, titles, order, sources, and transcript count. A transcript is current only when that check passes against the current notes.

## Delivery calibration

The delivery pattern was calibrated against classroom recordings from Harvard's Statistics 110 and MIT OpenCourseWare's 18.05: short orientation, a visible example before a formal step, explicit interpretation of graphs, and frequent local checks. The calibration affects pacing and explanation, not the empirical content of this linguistics course.
